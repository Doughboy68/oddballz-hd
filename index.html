    📖 THE RIFT

    A rift has torn open at the edge of known space, and it is leaking oddballz: unstable particles that drift loose in clusters of four.

    Nothing can close it, and nothing sent in comes back. You have taken the job anyway — a one-way run in a lone containment rig whose hexagonal field grid pulls the oddballz out of the dark towards it, faster the longer it holds. Every cluster it draws in is yours to clean up: as many as you can, before the field gives out.

    Line them up — by colour, or in full rows across the grid — and they cancel out. Let them pile up and the field collapses, and with it the mission. What you leave behind is the count: how much you cleared, and how far you got.

    Some particles are stranger than others. A superposition is every colour at once until it is matched. Charged particles discharge along the grid’s bottom row. Unstable cores go off where they land, taking their neighbours with them. And dead matter never reacts to a match — only a core or a discharge clears it, and if it falls it crushes whatever it lands on.

    Seven sectors lie ahead: the Cosmic Stellar Nebula, the Cyber Conduit Spaceframe, the Quantum Hyperspace Warp, the Alien Bio-Reactor Core, the Deep Space Citadel, the Orbital Solar Array and the Ion Storm. There is no way home through any of them, but each one you reach fires a containment pulse that skims the top off the pile. Make every one count.

    ℹ️ ABOUT & CREDITS

    The gameplay and the original Borland Pascal engine are Fred Kohler's. Brian Semotiuk converted the game to Windows, adapting the code where the port required it, working on it from around 1992–93 and finalising the build in 1994. It was never fully finished and was never released. Quantum Oddballz ports that engine to JavaScript, with the aim of finally finishing what was started over thirty years ago.

    The matching, support and gravity rules are the original's, untouched. Around them: selectable board sizes, a game timer, high scores kept separately per mode and board, reworked levelling and scoring, and a number of long-standing bugs fixed. The presentation is rebuilt throughout — real-time 3D sphere physics, metallic shaders, dynamic space flight backdrops, Web Audio synthesis, smooth continuous motion in place of the original's step-by-step movement, and pieces that flip in place rather than pivoting end over end about a single ball.

    Also in Quantum Oddballz: superpositions, unstable cores, dead matter and charged particles among the balls, eight original songs, seven animated space sectors drawn live, a next piece preview and a ghost preview.
